# Very brief summary of occupancy analyses — 26/08

*Supersedes the 21/08 and 23/08 notes. The change since then is not new data but
a **correction**: ProteinMPNN was being read at the wrong residue for a quarter
of sites, and fixing it reverses the headline. Two context experiments have also
been run and completed.*

---

## The main result has changed

**ProteinMPNN does distinguish occupied sequons from matched controls on the
conditional score.** In the largest and most relevant secretory comparison it
scores **+0.282 SD** (permutation p = 0.008, BH 0.031, Holm 0.031) across 232
pairs and 70 resampling units. The 21/08 figure of +0.09 SD, with an interval
crossing zero, was an artefact.

The defect: the manifest's residue indices count observed residues, while
ProteinMPNN's parser walks the author numbering and inserts a placeholder for
every absent residue number. The two coincide only for chains numbered without
gaps. **25.3% of sites were read at the wrong residue**, and a further 8.8%
cannot be reconciled and are now refused. ESM-IF and ESMC were never affected —
both already reconciled the two parses and checked the residues they landed on;
the ProteinMPNN adapter accepted the same guard argument and ignored it. Full
account in [`correction_2026-08-25_sequon_indexing.md`](correction_2026-08-25_sequon_indexing.md).

That ESM-IF rerun through the corrected code reproduces **+0.431 SD exactly** is
what makes the ProteinMPNN change attributable: both models were rerun, and only
the defective one moved.

## Where the four comparisons now sit

| Comparison | ProteinMPNN | ESM-IF1 | ESMC single | ESMC joint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **secretory** | **+0.282** (BH 0.031) | **+0.431** (BH 0.0008) | **+0.261** | −0.113 |
| internal control (n=15) | −0.386 | −0.337 | +0.792 | +0.711 |
| bacterial *(diagnostic)* | −0.528 | −0.265 | +0.416 | +0.050 |
| cytosolic *(diagnostic)* | −0.002 | +0.121 | −0.132 | −0.529 |

**Correction now spans the confirmatory comparisons only.** Bacterial and
cytosolic sequons cannot be occupied — wrong compartment, no
oligosaccharyltransferase — so a test against them cannot answer the question and
does not belong in a family whose purpose is to price the chances a real answer
had to appear. They are still computed and reported, uncorrected.

That change cost something under BH and it is worth knowing why: those two
diagnostics were reliably significant, so they took the lowest ranks and loosened
the threshold above them. The secretory score reads BH 0.021 in a family of eight
and 0.031 in a family of four. The help was illegitimate — BH controls a
false-discovery proportion, and a family stuffed with guaranteed discoveries
inflates its estimate of how many are real.

## Retention is complete, and does not follow the score

| Comparison | ESM-IF1 | ProteinMPNN |
|---|---|---|
| eukaryotic secretory | **+0.0925** (BH 0.015) | +0.0700 (BH 0.225) |
| internal control | −0.023 | −0.081 |

ProteinMPNN's retention moved the same way as its score after the correction
(+0.0423 to +0.0700) without clearing. Its permutation p is 0.112 against a
Wilcoxon p of 0.003 — that order-of-magnitude gap is the ortholog clustering, and
the permutation test is the pre-specified one.

## The baseline, restated

The 21/08 note said ProteinMPNN provided "a largely negative baseline". That is
no longer true, and the revised picture is less interesting rather than more:

- **Three of three models detect something** on the best-powered comparison, at
  small effect sizes — occupied sites are preferred in 59% of pairs by
  ProteinMPNN and 64% by ESM-IF, against a coin flip at 50%.
- **The architecture contrast has largely dissolved.** "ESM-IF discriminates
  where ProteinMPNN does not" was the interesting claim, and it was an indexing
  artefact. What remains is two models doing the same weak thing at somewhat
  different strengths.
- **Jointly masked ESMC still loses its effect** (+0.12 to +0.007; change +0.113,
  95% CI [+0.071, +0.147]), so its single-mask signal depends on the interaction
  between visible motif and context rather than on context alone. That result is
  unaffected by the correction.
- **The ProteinMPNN joint-mask row is stale** and must not be interpreted: it
  predates the indexing correction and has not been regenerated.
- **The internal-control comparison is null on both outcomes**, at 15 pairs. It
  carries the cleanest label and cannot settle anything.
- **The diagnostics still do not form an interpretable ordering**, so the
  triangulation argument the earlier notes rested on remains unavailable.

## Two context experiments, both complete

**Does fixing the motif protect the biology around it?** Over 285 sites,
protected-sequon designs drift from natural occupied context (+0.050
[0.033, 0.067]) and further than a composition-matched random control (+0.033
[0.020, 0.046]). But a composition control shows the shift — more proline and
glycine, fewer aromatics — is **global to the chain**, slightly weaker near the
sequon than elsewhere. So: holding N-X-S/T fixed does not hold its surroundings
fixed, because redesign changes composition everywhere. It is not a
glycosylation-specific failure.

**Are sequons lost faster than anything else?** No detectable excess. Exact
triplet retention is 7.6% at sequons against 9.5% at three-residue windows
elsewhere in the same chains; the difference is 1.8 pp with an interval spanning
zero — though reaching +4.6 pp, so this is not a demonstration of equality. It
does **not** show the loss is "general redesign rather than glycan blindness":
those are not alternatives, since a blind model would be expected to lose the
motif at the ordinary rate. What it shows is that ProteinMPNN does not
selectively protect the motif, and treats a known biological requirement as
ordinary mutable sequence.

## What this means for the question the benchmark was built to answer

The bar a glycosylation-aware model has to beat is now **higher and better
defined**: ESM-IF's +0.431 SD, not a null. A convincing improvement would still
need a stronger positive effect on the same frozen pairs, consistency across NXS
and NXT, survival of the control analyses, and sensible behaviour under joint
masking.

What has not changed is the ceiling on interpretation. The controls are
unannotated rather than known-negative, the comparison remains an
occupancy-associated preference under a proxy label, and nothing here has been
tested experimentally. The corrections have made the measurement trustworthy;
they have not made the labels better.
