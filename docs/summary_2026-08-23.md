> `model_index` counts observed residues; ProteinMPNN's parser walks the author
> numbering and inserts a placeholder for every absent number, so ProteinMPNN was
> read at the wrong residue for **25.3%** of sites. Corrected, its conditional
> score on the secretory comparison moves from **+0.090 SD (BH 0.30)** to
> **+0.282 SD (BH 0.021)**, and the claim that *ProteinMPNN does not distinguish
> occupied sequons* does not survive. Retention moves the same way (+0.0423 ->
> +0.0700) but does not clear correction. **ESM-IF and ESMC are unaffected** —
> ESM-IF reproduces +0.431 SD exactly. Any ProteinMPNN number below this line
> predates the fix. Full account:
> [`correction_2026-08-25_sequon_indexing.md`](correction_2026-08-25_sequon_indexing.md).

*Updates the 21/08 note. The change since then: **design retention now exists for
both models**, so every comparison has two outcomes rather than one, and all
p-values are corrected across the full family of eight tests rather than four.*

---

**ProteinMPNN still does not distinguish occupied sequons from matched controls —
on either outcome.** In the largest and most relevant secretory comparison it
scores +0.09 SD and retains the motif +0.042 more often, and neither survives
correction (BH 0.30 for both). The cytosolic comparison is near zero, the
bacterial comparison is negative, and the small internal-control comparison is
positive but extremely uncertain.

**ESM-IF1 does distinguish them, on both outcomes.** In the same secretory
comparison it scores **+0.431 SD** (BH 0.0008) and retains the motif
**+0.0925** more often — 15.1% vs 5.9% — (BH 0.015). Both clear correction across
all eight tests.

So the baseline is no longer uniformly negative. It is **architecture-dependent**.

| Retention, secretory | **+0.0925** (BH 0.015) | +0.0423 (BH 0.30) |

**The direction agrees everywhere.** Both models, both outcomes: positive on
secretory, negative on bacterial. ESM-IF is not finding something ProteinMPNN
contradicts — it is resolving something ProteinMPNN also weakly shows.

The benchmark asks whether a model supports the motif more strongly when it sits
in the sequence and structural context of an occupied site. The answer is now
*yes for ESM-IF, not detectably for ProteinMPNN*.

## What masking adds

Both arms of every comparison contain the motif, so motif recognition alone
cannot separate them. Masking the whole sequon asks what the **surroundings
alone** say.

**Sequence-only ESMC loses its effect entirely.** Occupied sequons are preferred
over matched controls by +0.12 in predicted probability; that falls to +0.007
when the motif is hidden — a change of **+0.113, 95% CI [+0.071, +0.147],
p < 0.0001** over 72 independent units. Its signal depended on the interaction
between the visible motif and its context.

![](../results/figures/current/fig3_convergence.png)

**ProteinMPNN's does not.** The same test gives a change of **−0.007**
[−0.016, −0.002] — sixteen times smaller and in the opposite direction. Its
(weak) discrimination does not require seeing the motif.

So the two conditioning schemes behave qualitatively differently, which is
itself a result: the sequence-only model judges motif-in-context, while the
structure-conditioned model does not depend on the motif being visible.

*(ESM-IF has no joint-masking variant yet — a causal decoder cannot hide an
upstream residue without also hiding it from everything downstream. That is the
main gap in this analysis.)*

---

## The benchmark baseline

* Motif recognition alone is insufficient, because both groups contain the motif.
* **ProteinMPNN gives a negative baseline** for occupancy discrimination, on both
  outcomes.
* **ESM-IF gives a positive one**, on both outcomes, surviving correction — so
  "current models are glycosylation-blind" is too strong a claim.
* Different architectures and conditioning schemes detect different contextual
  signals, and masking separates which.
* The controls do their job: bacterial fires negatively in **every** cell, which
  says structural matching alone does not remove compartment and composition
  differences.

## Caveats worth stating

* **The internal-control comparison is null in all four cells.** It carries the
  cleanest label — annotated absence rather than absence of annotation — and it
  supports nothing. At n=16 it cannot refute the secretory result, but it does
  not back it.
* **The secretory control is contaminated by construction** (~half of eukaryotic
  secretory proteins with structures carry the glycoprotein keyword).
  Contamination pulls toward the null, so it cannot manufacture the effect — the
  true effect is likely larger — but the label is "unannotated", not "annotated
  negative".
* **Nothing was pre-registered**, and the secretory comparison is the same set
  that generated the earlier retention lead, so it is not independent
  confirmation of it.
* A scoring defect was found and fixed on 20/08 (the ProteinMPNN token alphabet
  was misindexed, so one term read aspartate rather than asparagine). All scores
  and all retention were regenerated. It changed magnitudes, not conclusions.

## Next

We can now ask whether a deliberately glycosylation-aware model improves on this
baseline. A convincing improvement would produce a stronger positive
occupied-versus-control effect **on the same frozen pairs**, hold across NXS and
NXT, survive the control analyses, and — the sharper test — behave sensibly under
joint masking, which distinguishes an improvement that comes from wider context
from one that comes from how the model integrates motif with context.

The comparison that matters is **the same model before and after glycosylation
training**, not one architecture against another — otherwise architecture, scale
and training data are confounded. ESM-IF now sets the bar to beat.
